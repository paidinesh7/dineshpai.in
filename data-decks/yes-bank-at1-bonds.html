What Is an AT1 Bond?

After the 2008 crisis, regulators asked banks to issue special bonds (Additional Tier-1) that can take losses if a bank fails
BANK INSTRUMENT END DATE TYPICAL RETURN IF THE BANK GETS INTO TROUBLE LOSS ORDER
Fixed Deposit (FD) 1 to 5 years 6.5% – 7.5% Protected first (insured up to ₹5 lakh) Last to lose
Regular Bond (Tier-2) 5 to 10 years 7.5% – 8.0% Loses money only if the bank shuts down 3rd to lose
AT1 Bond (Perpetual) No end date 8.5% – 9.5% Bank can skip interest or write down to zero 2nd to lose
Bank Shares (Equity) No end date No fixed return Supposed to take the first full loss 1st to lose
Why do banks issue them? Almost every large Indian bank (SBI, HDFC Bank, Bank of Baroda, PNB) issues AT1 bonds because RBI counts them as core bank capital without the bank having to issue new shares. Over ₹1 lakh crore of AT1 bonds are active in India today.
Source: RBI Basel III Capital Regulations on Additional Tier-1 Instruments 02 / 05

What Happened at Yes Bank in March 2020

When Yes Bank nearly failed in March 2020, ₹8,415 crore of AT1 bonds were written down to zero
01 · WHO HELD THESE BONDS
Sold to Savers as "Super FDs"

Mutual funds (Nippon, Kotak, Franklin) and companies (like 63 Moons) held most of the bonds. But Yes Bank staff also sold ~₹679 Cr directly to over 1,300 individuals and retirees as safe "Super FDs" paying 9.5%.

02 · LOSS ORDER FLIPPED
Shareholders Kept Stock, Bondholders Got Zero

Normally, shareholders lose everything before any bondholder loses a rupee. At Yes Bank, AT1 bondholders lost 100% (₹8,415 Cr), while existing shareholders kept their shares (with a 3-year lock-in on 75% of stock).

03 · THE LEGAL MISTAKE
Written Off a Day Too Late

RBI's draft rescue plan on March 6 included writing off the AT1 bonds. When the Government notified the final law on March 13, that write-off clause was left out. Yet on March 14, the Administrator wrote them off anyway.

Source: SEBI Order on Yes Bank AT1 Mis-selling & Bombay High Court Judgment (Jan 2023) 03 / 05

What the Supreme Court Ruled Yesterday

The Court upheld the 2023 Bombay High Court order cancelling the ₹8,415 crore write-off
THE 3 DATES THAT DECIDED THE CASE
March 6, 2020 · RBI Draft Plan
Proposes writing down ₹8,415 Cr of AT1 bonds to zero.
March 13, 2020 · Final Govt Notification
Government notifies the final law and leaves out the AT1 write-off clause.
March 14, 2020 · Administrator's Order
A day after the final law took effect, the Administrator writes off the bonds anyway.
The Supreme Court ruled that once the final scheme took effect on March 13 without that clause, the Administrator had no legal power left on March 14 to wipe out the bonds.
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YES BANK
₹8,415 Cr Restored
The written-off bond liability returns to Yes Bank's books, which is why the stock fell over 15% yesterday.
Can Yes Bank handle this now?
In 2020, Yes Bank was out of cash. Today it is profitable (~₹2,400+ Cr annual profit), has over ₹45,000 Cr in net worth, and is backed by Japan's SMBC (24.2% stake). It lowers the bank's capital buffer, but does not put the bank at risk.
Source: Supreme Court Bench of Justices Dipankar Datta & Augustine George Masih (Oct 15, 2026) 04 / 05

What Changed After 2020

How SEBI changed the rules so retail savers cannot easily buy AT1 bonds anymore
01 · MINIMUM LOT SIZE
Raised From ₹10 Lakh to ₹1 Crore

SEBI raised the minimum investment size for AT1 bonds from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore and restricted trading to institutional platforms. Regular retail savers can no longer buy them directly.

02 · MUTUAL FUND RULES
10% Cap on Debt Funds

Several debt mutual funds lost money in 2020 because they held Yes Bank AT1 bonds. SEBI capped AT1 bond holdings at 10% of any mutual fund scheme and tightened how funds value them.

03 · NOT JUST INDIA
Credit Suisse Wiped Out $17B in 2023

Three years after Yes Bank, Switzerland did the exact same thing when UBS rescued Credit Suisse in 2023: $17 billion of AT1 bonds were written down to zero while shareholders still got $3.2 billion.

Source: SEBI Circulars on Perpetual Debt Instruments (Oct 2020 & Mar 2021) 05 / 05
