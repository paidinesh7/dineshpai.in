Thesis Investments

I grew up by the River Seetha in Udupi. After studying computer science at RVCE (2014), I started my career in financial research at D.E. Shaw India and later completed my MBA at the University of Calgary.

Along the way, I built and shut down two small businesses—a textbook delivery service in Bangalore and a student housing venture in Canada. Dealing firsthand with working capital cycles, vendor terms, and thin operating margins taught me more about business mortality than any financial model.

Since 2020, I have been at Zerodha, where I lead private market investments at Rainmatter and work on long-term ecological and rural livelihood initiatives through the Rainmatter Foundation.

How I Invest

Because Rainmatter invests solely from Zerodha's balance sheet with no external LPs or fixed fund lifecycles, we can underwrite 15- to 30-year compounding without forcing premature exits. When backing early-stage founders, my focus comes down to four principles:

  • Unit Economics Before Scale: Growth without contribution margin only magnifies fragility. We back businesses where unit economics work early and customer acquisition isn't subsidized by venture capital.
  • Structural Counter-Positioning: Business models that incumbents cannot easily copy without undercutting their own core revenue streams.
  • Hard Moats & Patient Capital: Across fintech, climate, healthcare, and Indian deeptech, we prefer backing proprietary engineering, physical supply chains, and regulatory infrastructure that take years to replicate.
  • Capital Discipline & Alignment: Founders who treat equity as their most expensive asset, keep fixed costs lean, and build trust with customers rather than optimizing for the next valuation markup.

If you are building with a similar long-term lens, reach out.

The Weekly Signal

Reading notes • Capital & markets

Three papers, essays, or discussions from my reading each week—and how they connect to investing and building in India.

Week of October 12, 2026

  • Venture Capital • The Odin Times (Dan Gray)
    The Importance of Playing to Win →

    In venture capital, chasing 'access' to hot, consensus deals is usually a trap. The best founders and investors learn faster from direct, real-world feedback than from polished credentials.

  • Business Models • Collaborative Fund (Craig Shapiro)
    The Best Charity of the Next 50 Years Might Be a Company →

    For a century, companies extracted profits first and gave back through charity later. The most enduring businesses of the next 50 years will build models where unit economics improve only when customer and community outcomes improve.

  • Behavioral Finance • Behavioural Investment (Joe Wiggins)
    Why Doesn’t the Risk of AI Feel More Frightening? →

    We are wired to react to vivid, emotional threats while ignoring slow-moving statistical risks. In markets as in technology, abstract risks on a spreadsheet rarely feel real until the damage is already done.

Week of October 5, 2026

  • Business Moats • Pivotal
    Trust Is The Scarce Asset →

    As AI makes generating code and content nearly free, value shifts to what cannot be automated: trust. Durable moats will belong to businesses built on skin in the game, reputation, and clear accountability.

  • Fintech • Open Standard (Visa / Stripe)
    The Stablecoin Inversion: OUSD & The Open Standard Coalition →

    Instead of keeping interest income on reserves like Tether or Circle, Open USD shares 100% of yield and network ownership with the platforms distributing it—a modern take on how Visa was originally built as a member-owned network.

  • Product Strategy • Build with AI (GrowthX)
    RIP, the Product Playbook →

    When software can take a user from prompt to output in seconds, traditional onboarding funnels stop mattering. The real bottleneck shifts from building features to having clear product judgment.

Week of September 19, 2026

  • Risk Management • Moontower Meta
    after this post you will be sizing bets in your head →

    Kris Abdelmessih breaks down position sizing under uncertainty—why even a great bet can ruin you if you size it too aggressively, and why survival always comes before maximizing returns.

  • Fintech • fintechbrainfood.com
    Nubank America & The AWS of Money →

    How Nubank is entering the U.S. market through partner banking rails and using low-cost cross-border settlement to connect everyday retail banking with global transfers.

  • Livelihoods & AI • New York Times
    Kenyans Did Homework for Years. Then A.I. Arrived. →

    A sobering look at how generative AI wiped out a 40,000-person freelance writing economy in Nairobi almost overnight—and a reminder of how fragile purely transactional digital work has become.

Week of September 12, 2026

  • AI Policy • darioamodei.com
    We Must Pace the Frontier →

    Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei makes the case for tying frontier AI deployment speed to measurable safety benchmarks rather than racing blindly ahead.

  • Governance • Business Standard
    The Dauphin Discount: Love the Founder →

    Why public markets often price in a 15–25% discount when second-generation heirs take over from founders—and how hard it is to pass down founder-level urgency and cost discipline.

  • Capital Markets • A Wealth of Common Sense
    A Short History of Trend-Following and Momentum →

    A clear look at why momentum persists across centuries of market data, and how simple index funds quietly act as long-term trend-following machines.

Week of September 5, 2026

  • Early Stage • paulgraham.com
    Do Things That Don't Scale →

    Paul Graham's classic reminder that startups don't take off on their own—in the beginning, you have to recruit users manually, solve their problems by hand, and resist automating too early.

  • Strategy • notboring.co
    An Ode to Counter-Positioning →

    How challengers win against giants by choosing a business model that incumbents cannot copy without hurting their own existing cash cow.

  • Behavioral Finance • Morgan Housel
    The Psychology of Money →

    Doing well with money has little to do with how smart you are and everything to do with how you behave—staying unbreakable so you can let compounding work uninterrupted.
