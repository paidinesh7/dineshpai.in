Was at the Basecamp inauguration yesterday. And I met with quite a few people. A few were building companies, a few were there to learn and see if they should build a company, and a few were there to support the ecosystem. But a few conversations stuck with me. And I felt it might be useful to share this so that, when you are attending these events, it is helpful to you.

## Events are the worst place to pitch your business

To be really honest, at events, beyond greeting people and being glad to catch up personally with folks I know or people I am meeting for the first time, it is really tough to hear out, debate, or share opinions on anything at all.

Think about it. I'm constantly distracted. My phone has buzzed 10 times. Whatever you tell me about your company in that state, I will half-hear and fully forget by morning.

So, don't pitch. Just say hi. Make it a real hello, who you are, what you're building in one line, and then a genuine question about them or the event. That's it. Keep it super simple.

If we have a warm two-minute conversation, I will for sure remember you fondly. And a fond memory gets a reply to your email on Monday. A pitch I couldn't absorb makes me more confused.

## Ask one sharp question

The most common question I get is some type of "what should I do?" And I for the life of me cant answer it really well, every time. Because a really thoughtful answer takes quite a bit of time to think through. I don't know your context, your constraints, your co-founders, or risk. So I am bound to say something generic and useless. Or, in other words, I will say something stupid.

Compare that to someone who asks a simple and pointed thing. For example, "we're deciding between marketing with x channel or y channel, what has been your personal experience as a customer?" Or "have you seen portfolio companies work through this problem?" I can answer that.

So probably bring one sharp question. And the good news is that a narrow question usually opens the big conversation anyway.

## Spend your time with the other founders, not the investors

This is the one I'd most want a young people at events to hear.

Everyone at these events constantly seeks out the investors. Founders queue to talk to them. I understand the instinct. But I think most are missing a trick.

The best stories in any of these rooms belong to the entrepreneurs. The ones who kept a company alive with no money. Who fired a close colleague. Who watched a launch flop and figured out why. Who solved the exact distribution problem you're chasing.

That is the specific, expensive knowledge you should seek out, and these fellow founders will usually give it to you because they will love speaking about how they overcame challenges. An investor can tell you what patterns they are seeing. But a founder two years ahead of you can tell you what it actually felt like. Like how Mastercard puts it, its priceless.
